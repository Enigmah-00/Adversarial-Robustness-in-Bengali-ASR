==============================================================================
  1 - how much of sigma is the ATTACK, not the training?
==============================================================================

  The published sigma treats every difference between two runs of one condition
  as training variance. It is not: each run was attacked once, so the figure also
  contains the variance of the attack itself. Where the same weights were
  attacked twice, that component is directly visible.

  1h run2        same weights, attacked twice: 41.0% and 61.0%  ->  20.0 pp apart

  attack-measurement sd = 14.14 pp raw, 17.72 pp bias-corrected (df 1)
  df 1 is ONE pair. The point estimate is near-worthless on its own; what
  it establishes is that the component is not negligible.

--- implied split, total sigma = 21.574 pp ----------------------------------
  attack sd | training sd | share of VARIANCE that is attack noise
      8.0 pp |    20.04 pp  |   14%
     12.0 pp |    17.93 pp  |   31%
     17.7 pp |    12.30 pp  |   67%   <- measured, df 1
     16.0 pp |    14.47 pp  |   55%
     20.0 pp |     8.09 pp  |   86%

  Read sigma as an UPPER BOUND on run-to-run variance. The two-run contrast that
  the matched pairs rely on still carries sigma*sqrt(2) = 30.51 pp whichever way
  the split falls, so the negative result does not depend on the decomposition --
  but the attribution to "retraining" does, and cannot be sustained.

  2 - sigma depends on the pooling choice; here are all three
==============================================================================
  pooling                                  raw sd   df     sigma sigma*sqrt2
  within (group, environment)  [reported]  20.279    4   21.574      30.51
  by rung, pooling environments           15.802    7   16.375      23.16
  seed-contrast groups only               18.859    3   20.470      28.95

  Each matched-pair effect against each noise estimate:
  pooling                                40x/38pp 16x/33pp  4x/13pp
  within (group, environment)  [reported]    1.25x    1.08x    0.43x
  by rung, pooling environments             1.64x    1.43x    0.56x
  seed-contrast groups only                 1.31x    1.14x    0.45x

  No pair reaches 2x the noise under any choice, so "not distinguishable" holds
  throughout -- but it holds by a wide margin under the reported estimate and a
  narrow one under the by-rung estimate. The correct wording is "cannot be
  distinguished given our noise estimate", not "is noise".

  The by-rung estimate has more df and is the more efficient one. It is not the
  headline because each pair it adds differs in LIBRARY VERSION as well as in
  run, so it mixes run-to-run variance with a 4.44.2-to-5.16.1 difference. That
  is a real reason, and it is now stated rather than left implicit.

==============================================================================
  5 - what happens without the 100 h milestones, and with a version term
==============================================================================
  The 100 h milestones are the top of the exposure range and the most
  leveraged points in the slope. They are checkpoints at steps 1000, 2000 and
  3500 of 2 training run(s): 100h-r1, 100h-s1338. Within every such run, exposure is
  perfectly collinear with training step, so more audio and more optimisation
  cannot be told apart. A second run adds an independent cluster; it does NOT
  break that collinearity, because both runs were checkpointed at the same steps.
  The version indicator below is pre/post the library upgrade (4.44.2 vs later).
  all 16 conditions [reported]       beta1 +0.0888  se 0.0294  df 11  CI [+0.0240, +0.1535]  excludes 0
  10 replicate runs, no milestones   beta1 +0.6473  se 0.3904  df  9  CI [-0.2357, +1.5303]  INCLUDES 0
  + library-version indicator        beta1 +0.0885  se 0.0289  df 11  CI [+0.0249, +0.1522]  excludes 0

  Exposure in the no-milestone fit spans 0.98 to 24.6 h, a factor of 25 rather
  than 100, so a wider interval there is expected and is not by itself evidence
  against the slope. What matters is whether the sign and rough size survive.

==============================================================================
  6 - sensitivity to the imputation ceiling
==============================================================================
  679 of 1600 observations are right-censored (42.4%) and imputed at 2e-03.
  The cheap check here is whether the slope depends on where the ceiling is
  put; section 6b models the censoring directly.

  ceiling 2e-03  beta1 +0.0888  CI [+0.0240, +0.1535]  excludes 0
  ceiling 3e-03  beta1 +0.1093  CI [+0.0295, +0.1892]  excludes 0
  ceiling 5e-03  beta1 +0.1353  CI [+0.0361, +0.2344]  excludes 0
  ceiling 1e-02  beta1 +0.1704  CI [+0.0448, +0.2960]  excludes 0

  The ceiling is a lower bound on the censored values, so every estimate
  here is conservative with respect to the true (larger) eps50.

--- 6b - censored-normal (Tobit) fit, clustered on the training run ---------
  Instead of imputing, model the censoring. log10(eps50) is normal around the
  linear predictor; a row that never reached its target contributes
  P(y > log10 ceiling), and a success in the lowest bisection step, whose true
  value may lie below the 1e-4 floor, contributes P(y < that step's upper edge).
  Standard errors are a sandwich clustered on the training run.
  917 exact | 679 right-censored at 2e-03 | 4 left-censored below 1.0981e-04
  converged in 7 iterations, log-likelihood -1097.979
  beta1 = +0.1562 per decade   cluster se 0.0450 (model se 0.0186)   df 11
  95% CI [+0.0571, +0.2554]  excludes 0   ->  eps50 x 1.43 per decade [1.14, 1.80]
  WER coefficient +1.0319   residual sd 0.4528 (log10 units)
  imputed OLS (section 3) for comparison: beta1 +0.0888

  Modelling the censoring raises the slope above the imputed fit, which is
  what section 6 implies: imputing at the ceiling understates it.
  The cluster-robust se at this G is optimistic for the same reason as CR1;
  read this as a sensitivity check, not a replacement for the bootstrap.

==============================================================================
  8 - the unmatched ladder: single runs against replicate means
==============================================================================
  The paper argues the unmatched comparison 'points the wrong way' from
  75%% at 1 h against 79%% at 5 h. That is a 4-point gap, far inside sigma. With
  every run of each rung averaged, the same argument is made by a much wider
  margin.

  rung   runs                             single     mean
  1      1h final, 1h run2, 1h s1338, 1h s1339    75.0%    53.0%
  5      5h final, 5h s1338, 5h s1339      79.0%    78.0%
  25     25h final, 25h s1338, 25h s1339    66.0%    67.0%

  1 h -> 5 h as published:        +4.0 pp  (75.0 -> 79.0)
  1 h -> 5 h on replicate means:  +25.0 pp  (53.0 -> 78.0)
  The conclusion is unchanged and the evidence is 6.2x stronger.

  Everything above is CPU-only and reproduces from the committed CSVs.
  What it cannot do, and what therefore belongs in Limitations:

    * separate training from attack variance with any precision. One re-attacked
      checkpoint gives df 1. Three checkpoints at three PGD seeds would give a
      usable interval, at roughly 10 GPU-hours.
    * break the collinearity between exposure and training step at the top of
      the range. A second 100-hour run (now included, if INCLUDE_100H_REPLICATE
      is on) adds an independent cluster but does NOT break it: both runs were
      checkpointed at the same steps. That needs checkpoints matched on
      optimisation but not on audio.
    * test attack strength properly: step size scaled with epsilon, restarts on
      failures, longer budgets. That would change every eps50 value, so it is a
      full re-measurement rather than an addition.
    * a listening test for perceptibility. SNR is reported instead.
